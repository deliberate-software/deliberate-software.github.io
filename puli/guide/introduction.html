Puli · User’s Guide

Introducing Puli.

Most notes apps ask where a note belongs. Puli asks who and what it’s about. Tag them as you write, and your notes connect themselves.

One note, everywhere it belongs

A note from a meeting is rarely about one thing. It’s about the people in the room, the teams they’re on, and the projects they discussed. A folder can keep it in only one of those places.

In Puli, you don’t file notes. You tag them. Write @Diane-Okafor, !Development, and #Grant-Report in a note, and it shows up on Diane’s page, the Development team’s page, and the Grant Report’s page. One note, in three places.

Tags connect your notes

Write about Diane in January, March, and June, and her page gathers all three, newest first. Her page also shows who and what she’s mentioned with: the people she works with, and the projects that keep coming up. You never set any of that up. It comes from the way you write.

Context, when you need it

Before a meeting with Diane, start a note and type @Diane-Okafor. Her page opens beside your note, with your notes from last time. Move the cursor to #Grant-Report, and its page takes Diane’s place. You don’t search, and you don’t switch windows.

Every promise, kept

Meetings end in promises: what you said you’d do, and what others said they’d do for you. Write either as a task, right in your note:

  • - [ ] Send @Diane-Okafor the #Grant-Report update by Thu is yours.
  • - [ ] @Priya-Shah to send the #Grant-Report outcomes is Priya’s.

With the Tasks lesson, Puli gathers them all in one place, and each person’s page shows what’s open between you.
