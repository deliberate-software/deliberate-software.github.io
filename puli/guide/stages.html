Puli · User’s Guide

Projects and stages.

Give your projects stages, like To do, Doing, and Done, and track their progress on a board.

Stages

A stage says where a project is. In each journal, one kind can have stages: Projects, in the Work, Home, and School templates, or Roles, in the Job search template.

A Job search journal starts with stages for its roles: Networking, Screening, Interviewing, Negotiating, and Closed. The other templates start with none. You add them when you want them.

Adding stages

  1. Choose Puli › Settings…, or press ⌘,.
  2. Choose Journal, then Tags.
  3. Beneath Projects’ sigil, or Roles’, click Add Stages.
  4. You see To do, Doing, and Done. Change their names, or leave them.
  5. Click Save.

Progress now shows in the cabinet and the View menu. Every project starts at the first stage until you choose another.

Changing stages

In the same place, you can rename a stage, move it up or down, remove it, or click Add Stage for another. Nothing changes until you click Save. Revert puts them back as they were. Each stage needs a name of its own, and a kind with stages needs at least one.

Renaming a stage keeps its projects in it. Your notes never change.

To stop using stages, click Remove Stages, then Save. Progress goes. Each project remembers the stage it was at, so if you add stages again, it’s back where it was.

In a Job search journal, Use the Template’s Stages puts back the stages it started with.

Choosing a project’s stage

Open the project’s page in the context pane. Its top line shows its kind and stage, as “Project · To do”. Click it and choose a stage. You can also drag the project’s card in Progress.

Changing a stage doesn’t change the project’s name, its About, or any note.

Priority from a stage

Each stage can have a priority, from P0, the highest, to P3, or None. Choose it beside the stage’s name in Settings › Journal › Tags.

An open task that tags a project takes the priority of the project’s stage. If the task tags several projects, it takes the highest. A priority you type in the task, like P0, comes first.

  • In the Tasks view, a priority from a stage shows in lighter text than one you typed.
  • Sorting or grouping by priority puts typed priorities before those from a stage.
  • Move the project to another stage, and its tasks’ priorities change with it. Your notes don’t.

A Job search journal’s stages start with priorities: Networking P3, Screening P2, Interviewing P1, Negotiating P0, and Closed none. A task about a role you’re interviewing for is P1. Change them as you like.

Tracking progress

Progress shows only in a journal where a kind has stages. To see it, choose View › Progress, press ⇧⌘P, or click Progress in the cabinet.

Progress is a board. Each stage is a column, in order, headed by its name, how many projects it holds, and its priority, if it has one. Each project is a card in its stage’s column. A card shows:

  • The project’s name.
  • The groups and people it’s mentioned with most, in their colors.
  • How many open tasks it has, and the highest priority among them, as “4 open tasks · P1”.
  • When the next task is due, as “next today”, “next in 3d”, or “next 18d late”.

Click a card to see the project’s page in the context pane. Click its open tasks to see the Tasks view, showing only that project’s tasks.

Moving cards

Drag a card to another column to change its stage. Drop it in a column’s empty space to put it at the bottom, or onto another card to put it just above that one. Within a column, Progress keeps the order you give it. A project that joins a column goes to the bottom.

The last column, like Done, shows only the projects mentioned or changed in the last 30 days, then “and 12 more”, say. Click that to see them all.

Projects inside projects

A project inside another, like #website/checkout, has its own card and its own stage. Its card shows the project it’s inside, small and muted, above its name. Moving one doesn’t move the other.

To see one project and everything inside it, click it in the cabinet. See Finding your notes.
