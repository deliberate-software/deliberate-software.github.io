Puli · Handler’s Guide

Fetching context.

The context pane sits beside your note. It shows the page of whoever or whatever you’re writing about: what you’ve said about them, and who they’re mentioned with.

Open a page

Each person, group, and project has a page. To see one in the context pane, click its tag in a note, or move the cursor onto the tag. You can also click a name in the sidebar, or on another page.

The page stays when you move on to text with no tag, so you can keep it beside you while you write.

What’s on a page

Under the name and its kind, a page shows:

  • About: a note of its own, for what you always want to remember, such as a person’s role or a project’s goal.
  • Lately: a few short points from its notes of the last two months, on a Mac with Apple Intelligence.
  • Mentioned with: the people, groups, and projects it comes up with.
  • Notes: every note that tags it, newest first. Click one to open it in the editor.

With the Tasks lesson, a page also shows its open tasks.

Click a section’s heading to close it, and again to open it.

Write an About

Choose Edit from the ⋮ beside the page’s name, and type in About. Tags and tasks work there as in any note. Click Done when you’re finished.

Go back and forward

Click ‹ and › above the context pane to go back to a page you saw before, and forward again.

Hide the context pane

Choose View › Hide Context, or press ⌘\. Press it again to bring the pane back. It also comes back when you click a tag.

Give it its own window

To keep the context pane on another display, or beside a video call, choose View › Move Context to Its Own Window, or press ⌥⌘\. Press it again, or close the window, to move it back.
