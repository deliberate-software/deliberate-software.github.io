Puli · User’s Guide

Getting started.

How to make your first journal, try a sample, find your way around, and write your first note.

The welcome window

The first time you open Puli, it says “Puli is ready when you are.” You can:

  • Start a New Journal, empty, for your own notes.
  • Open an Existing Journal you’ve made before, or one from iCloud.
  • Import Notes from Apple Notes, Bear, Agenda, Obsidian, or a folder. See Importing notes.
  • Try a Sample Journal, full of notes, with every lesson turned on.

After that, Puli opens the journal you last had open.

Start a journal

A journal holds your notes, and the people and things they’re about. Choose Start a New Journal, then a template:

  • Work: people, orgs, and projects.
  • Home: people, groups, and projects.
  • School: people, courses, and assignments.
  • Job search: people, employers, and roles. Today, Tasks, Progress, and Graph are free in it.

The template decides what you can tag. Give the journal a name, then choose Start writing.

Before you start, you can turn on Back up to iCloud, Require Touch ID, and Show in Spotlight and Siri. You can change each of them later in Settings. See Journals.

Try a sample

A sample is a journal someone has been keeping for weeks, so you can see Puli at work before you’ve written much. Choose Harbor Street, an operations lead at a nonprofit, or Job Search, a staff engineer looking for their next role.

Every lesson works in a sample. A sample isn’t saved: each one starts fresh. To open one later, choose Help › Try a Sample Journal…

Find your way around

Puli’s window has four parts, from left to right:

  • The sidebar holds the views, such as Notes and Tags, and everyone and everything your notes tag.
  • The list of notes, newest first.
  • The editor, where you write.
  • The context pane shows the page of whoever or whatever the cursor is on.

Use the buttons in the toolbar, or the View menu, to show or hide any of them.

Write your first note

Choose File › New Note, or press ⌘N. Type a title on the first line, press ↩, and write. Tag someone with @ and their name, such as @Diane-Okafor. Press ⌘S to save.
