Puli · User’s Guide

People, groups,
and projects.

Tag the @people, !groups, and #projects you write about, and Puli keeps track of them for you.

Tag someone

Type a sigil and a name. In a journal made with the Work template:

  • @Diane tags a person.
  • !Development tags a group: an organization, a team, the PTA.
  • #Grant-Report tags a project: anything you’re working on.

For a name with more than one word, join the words with hyphens: @Diane-Okafor, #Board-Retreat. Puli shows it as “Diane Okafor”.

Saving the note creates anyone and anything it tags that the journal doesn’t have yet. Each gets its own page, listing the notes that mention it. Click a tag to open its page in the context pane.

For a reminder of your journal’s tags, choose Help › Tagging.

What counts as a tag

A tag starts where a word does. These aren’t tags:

  • Email addresses, like diane@example.org, and anything in a web address.
  • Anything in code, between backticks or in a code block.
  • A heading’s #, followed by a space.
  • A number, like #2026.
  • A tag with a backslash before it, like \#Grant-Report.

Punctuation around a tag isn’t part of it, so “Thanks, @Diane.” tags Diane. Neither is a possessive: @O'Brien's report tags O’Brien, and @Tim'll tags Tim. Names in every script work, accents and all.

Tag names

Capitals don’t matter. @diane, @Diane, and @DIANE all mean the same person.

The name comes from the tag as you first write it. @diane-okafor becomes “Diane Okafor”, and !HR stays “HR”. Writing !hr later doesn’t change it. Once you rename one, its tag shows the new name in every note.

Each kind keeps its own names, so @Ellis, !Ellis, and #Ellis are a person, a group, and a project.

How tags look

Once the cursor moves away, a tag shows its name in bold, without its sigil, in its kind’s color. In the Work template, people are blue, groups green, and projects amber. Move the cursor back into a tag and it shows as you wrote it, so you can change it. Copying copies it as written.

A tag the journal doesn’t know yet looks like any other. At the bottom of the editor, Puli lists it as new.

Tags to check

At the bottom of the editor, Puli lists the tags in the note that need a decision. Click one to see its menu.

New

“Marc NEW” means the journal has no @marc. Its menu offers people with similar names, then Create a new person, then Not a tag.

  • Choose a person to use their tag instead. The note then says @Marco-Silva.
  • Choose Create a new person to add Marc now.
  • Choose Not a tag to keep @marc as plain text. Puli adds a backslash before it.

If you do nothing, saving the note creates Marc.

Same as?

“Grant Reports SAME AS?” means the tag looks like one the journal has, such as #grant-reports when you have the project “Grant Report”, or #grnat-report. Puli doesn’t create it when you save. Its menu starts with Use Grant Report, which changes the tag to the one you have, and Keep Both, which creates the new one.

Too deep

“TOO DEEP” means a project tag nests more levels than the journal allows. Saving doesn’t create it. See Nested projects, below.

Autocomplete

Type a sigil and Puli suggests that kind’s names, the ones you mention most first. Keep typing to narrow them. Suggestions match any part of a name, and allow for small slips, so @oka and @prya find Diane Okafor and Priya Shah.

  • ↓ moves to the next suggestion.
  • ↩, or a click, writes the tag.
  • ⇥ writes it with a space after it.
  • ⎋ closes the suggestions and keeps what you typed.

When two people share a name, each suggestion shows its tag, smaller and muted, so you can tell them apart. “Another Diane…” at the bottom writes @diane-, ready for a last name. When no one has the tag you typed, the last suggestion is “Dana · create new”. Tags you’ve already written in the note but not yet saved come first, marked NEW.

Aliases

An alias is another tag for the same person, group, or project. If Diane has the alias @DO, writing @DO tags Diane, and shows “Diane Okafor”. Typing @D suggests Diane, and writes @Diane-Okafor. Diane’s page lists the notes that use either tag, with “Also @DO” under her name.

To add one, open Diane’s page in the context pane, choose Edit from its ⋮, type the alias in Add alias, and press ↩. To remove one, click the ✕ on it. If notes use a deleted alias, Puli asks whether to change them to @Diane-Okafor or keep them as written. If you keep them, the alias becomes a person of its own.

An alias that already belongs to someone else is refused, with a message saying whose it is. Merging Dee into Diane Okafor also gives Diane the alias @Dee. See Fetching Context for merging.

Nested projects

Put a project inside another with a slash: #Board-Retreat/Dinner/Caterer is Caterer, inside Dinner, inside Board Retreat. It shows as “Caterer”. Saving creates any of the three the journal doesn’t have.

Projects nest up to 4 levels in the Work, Home, and School templates. Other kinds don’t nest, so !Ellis-Foundation/legal tags the Ellis Foundation, followed by “/legal”. In the Job search template, nothing nests.

Type #Board-Retreat/ to see only the projects inside Board Retreat. Suggestions show their parents after their names: “Caterer · Board Retreat / Dinner”. A project’s page shows what it’s Part of, and the projects inside it.

Kinds and templates

People are in every journal, always with @, always blue. The journal’s template names its other kinds, their sigils, and their colors:

  • Work: Orgs (!) and Projects (#).
  • Home: Groups (!) and Projects (#).
  • School: Courses (!) and Assignments (#).
  • Job search: Employers (!) and Roles (#).

You choose a template when you make a journal. Help › Tagging explains each kind in the template’s own words.

Suggested tags

Puli can also spot names the journal knows that you wrote without a sigil, and suggest tagging them. That’s a lesson: see Suggested tags.
