Puli · User’s Guide

People, groups, and projects.

How to tag who and what your notes are about, and keep your tags tidy.

Tag someone

Type @ and a person’s name, with hyphens for spaces: @Diane-Okafor. Puli shows it as a blue pill reading “Diane Okafor”. The first time you save a note that tags her, Diane joins the journal, with a page of her own.

As you type, Puli suggests the people you’ve tagged before. Press ↩, or click one, to use it.

Groups and projects

Each kind of tag has its own mark and color. In a Work journal:

  • @ for people, such as @Diane-Okafor, in blue.
  • ! for orgs, such as !Development, in plum.
  • # for projects, such as #Grant-Report, in green.

Other templates name the kinds for what they hold: groups in a Home journal, courses and assignments in a School journal, and employers and roles in a Job Search journal. To see your journal’s kinds and marks, choose Help › Tagging, or open Settings › Tags.

New tags

When you tag someone the journal doesn’t know yet, they show in the tag rail, below the editor, marked NEW. Click the pill to choose who you meant, create someone new, or say it’s not a tag. If you do nothing, saving creates them.

Names are unique. If you tag a new John Smith when you already have one, the rail says NAME TAKEN. Choose Use John Smith, or Another John Smith… to tell them apart, as in “John Smith (Acme)”.

Tag a word you already wrote

Right-click a name in a note. Choose Tag as and the name of someone the journal knows, or Tag as a New Person, a New Org, or a New Project. To make a tag plain text again, right-click it and choose Not a Tag.

Nested projects

A project can hold other projects. Write #Grant-Report/Budget, and Budget is part of Grant Report. A note about the budget shows on both pages.

To move a project inside another, right-click it in the sidebar and choose Nest Under. Choose Top Level to bring it back out.

Tidy your tags

Each tag has a page in the context pane. Its ⋮, beside the name, holds:

  • Edit, to rename it. Every note that tags it changes to match.
  • Change Type, to make a person a project, say.
  • Merge Into, to make two tags one. Merging changes your notes, and can’t be undone.
  • Archive, for someone or something you’re done with. Its notes stay, but it leaves your lists and suggestions. Choose Restore to bring it back.
  • Delete, which moves it to the Trash. Its tags in your notes become plain text.

You can do the same from a tag’s right-click menu in the sidebar, or in the Tags view.
