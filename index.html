What Puli does for you.

Herds your notes

Mention a person, organization, or project, and Puli rounds up notes under each of them. One note, everywhere it belongs.

Comes when you call

Whistle with an @. Mention @maya, and everything you’ve written about her comes running, right next to your note.

Guards every promise

Jot down “Send @tim the deck” or “@maya will send the numbers,” and Puli keeps watch over both so you know what you owe and who owes you.

Made on purpose.

Made for privacy

Not a setting, and not a feature. Nothing you write is sent to us or used to train anything. Everything Puli does, it does on your Mac.

Made for everyone

Everything works from the keyboard and with VoiceOver, and the text grows to the size you need.

Made distraction-free

No streaks, no badges, no barking for attention. Puli stays at your side, never underfoot, and never pulls you out of your train of thought.

Made to keep its word

Questions? Answered.

What’s a Puli?

A Hungarian herding dog, famous for its corded coat. Quick, clever, and bred to keep the flock together. Say it POO-lee.

Is Puli really free?

Yes. Really. Everything you need to take notes and stay organized is free, forever. Lessons are optional, and you can try them free for two weeks. How lessons work.

What do I keep if I stop paying for lessons?

Every lesson Puli learned while you subscribed, including the ones released before you started. Like the issues of National Geographic on your shelf, lessons are yours to keep after the subscription ends. How lessons work.

Where are my notes kept?

On your Mac, and if you choose, synced to your iCloud account, encrypted end to end. We never see them.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not yet. Puli starts on the Mac.

What Mac do I need?

Any Mac with Apple silicon, running macOS 26 or later.

Why I'm building Puli.

I’m a note taker. I’m guessing you are, too. And if you’re like me, you’ve probably tried every notes app there is, and never stuck with any of them.

Notion is too cumbersome to set up. Obsidian gives me control, but no opinions, so I have to do all the work. Bear came close, but there’s no sense of time. Agenda brings it all together, but it still makes me file each note in a single project. My notes don’t fit in one bucket.

So I built Puli for myself — for a job search, actually. I needed to track who I was talking to, at which companies, about which roles. I enjoyed using it so much, I wanted to use it for meetings at work.

Puli is fast. Puli is opinionated. It herds your notes so you don’t have to, fetches context for every situation, and watches everyone’s promises. It’s private. It’s inclusive. And it’s free! (Lessons just teach Puli a few more tricks.)

I hope Puli becomes your best friend, too.

Alan Ridlehoover
Founder, Deliberate Software
