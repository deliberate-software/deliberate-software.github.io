A look inside.

What Parker believes.

Your notes are yours

Every note is a Markdown file in a folder you choose, on your Mac or in your iCloud Drive, never on our servers. Open them in any editor, today or in twenty years.

Privacy is a given

Not a setting, and not a feature. Nothing you write is sent to us, sold, or used to train anything. When Parker answers a question, it answers on your Mac.

Made for everyone

Everything works from the keyboard, and the text grows to the size you need, from the editor to the context pane.

Calm, on purpose

No streaks, no badges, no nagging. Parker never pulls you out of your train of thought.

Questions.

Is Parker really free?

Yes. Everything you need to take notes and stay organized is free, with no time limit. Premium is optional, and you can try it free for two weeks.

What if I stop paying for Premium?

You keep what you have. Think of a National Geographic subscription: stop it, and the issues on your shelf are still yours. Every Premium feature released while you subscribe stays yours, for good.

Where are my notes kept?

In a folder you choose, on your Mac or in iCloud Drive, as Markdown files. Parker reads them where they are, and changes made in other apps show up right away.

Does Parker use AI?

Only when you ask it a question. Ask Parker uses Apple Intelligence on your Mac, so your notes never leave it. Everything else follows plain, predictable rules.

Is there an iPhone app?

Not yet. Parker starts on the Mac.

What Mac do I need?

Any Mac running macOS 15 or later. Ask Parker needs a Mac with Apple Intelligence.

Where Parker is headed.

Plans, not promises: what's coming in 1.0, what's next, and what's further off. Waitlist members hear about each step first, and their feedback helps set the order.

Why I'm building Parker.

I've kept notes in most of the apps you know. Notion never felt nimble to me: getting a note in and organized always felt cumbersome. Agenda feels much faster, and it knows your calendar, which is great, but your data is locked in their database. Obsidian is both fast and plain text, but it doesn't know your day or provide context for your next note.

I want all of it at once. Note taking that feels as fast as Obsidian. An app that knows my day, like Agenda. AI that helps without being in my face. And notes that live outside the editor, where I can use them as I see fit and keep them wherever I want.

I first designed Parker for a job search: every recruiter, company and role, kept straight without filing a thing. It worked so well that I wanted it at work, too.

Obsidian lets you take notes. Parker puts what you need beside the note in front of you, and learning your schedule is next on the roadmap.

Alan Ridlehoover
Founder, Deliberate Software
