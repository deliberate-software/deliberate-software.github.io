Notes that bring their own context.

You write it all down: the call with Tom, the budget thread, the year-end checklist. Then the meeting starts, and the notes you need are three searches away. Radar is a fast, plain-text notes app for Mac that files each note by who and what it's about, and keeps everything connected to it beside the page. It's all there before you even ask, so you walk in prepared.

Join the Radar waitlist How it works

Write fast. Tag as you go. See the context.

  1. 01

    Write it down

    Radar is built for speed: open it, type, move on. Getting a thought down should never feel cumbersome. And it should never require a mouse.

  2. 02

    Tag as you go

    Mark people with @, organizations with !, and projects with #, right in the text: @tom, !accounting, #year-end-2026. Tags aren't just labels. Each one is a first-class citizen with a note of its own.

  3. 03

    See the context

    Beside every note, Radar's context pane shows the tags, links and notes connected to it. Meeting @tom in !accounting about #year-end-2026? Your last conversation and the open questions are already there.

Why I'm building Radar.

I've kept notes in most of the apps you know. Notion never felt nimble to me: getting a note in and organized always felt cumbersome. Agenda feels much faster, and it knows your calendar, which is great, but your data is locked in their database. Obsidian is both fast and plain text, but it doesn't know your day or provide context for your next note.

I want all of it at once. Note taking that feels as fast as Obsidian. An app that knows my day, like Agenda. AI that helps without being in my face. And notes that live outside the editor, where I can use them as I see fit and keep them wherever I want.

I first designed Radar for a job search: every recruiter, company and role, kept straight without filing a thing. It worked so well that I wanted it at work, too.

Obsidian lets you take notes. Radar puts what you need beside the note in front of you, and learning your schedule is next on the roadmap.

Alan Ridlehoover
Founder, Deliberate Software
